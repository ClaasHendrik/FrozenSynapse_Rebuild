# Thin Ice: staying our own game (working checklist, not legal advice)

Principle: ideas, genres and general mechanics are free; specific expression is protected (code, art, audio, text, names, characters,
level layouts, distinctive UI layouts, exact data tables). Similarity by accident is low risk; copying from a reference is the risk.

1. Gameplay: plan-then-simultaneous-execute is a general idea. Do not copy a reference's exact order set, rule numbers, numeric tables,
   or signature mechanics. Keep our own: scale-changing campaign, intel and information asymmetry, morale levels, resolve and supply.
2. Interface: do not copy a reference's screen layout, icon set, panel arrangement or distinctive control scheme. Document our own.
3. Missions and story: written from scratch. Real history (Cold War, real places) is free; a reference's mission premises are not.
4. Names and titles: check the working title, unit names, faction names and slogans against trademark registers and app stores before
   committing; avoid anything close to a known game title. "Thin Ice" and subtitle both need a clearance search.
5. Art: all original, no tracing, no reference screenshots in the repo. Keep the production record of how art was made.
6. Audio and fonts: original or properly licensed; keep licence files. Fonts are a common trap.
7. Maps and data: Natural Earth is public domain; any other geodata, borders or icons need a licence check and attribution if required.
8. Real-world content: real weapon designations and military insignia are mostly fine; real people, parties and living figures are
   sensitive (defamation, rights of publicity). Fictional leaders with invented names. Handle real atrocities and wars with care.
9. Tools and libraries: engine licence (Godot is MIT), every library and AI-generated asset must have a licence that allows commercial
   release. Check the rules on AI-generated assets for the chosen store and for copyright registrability.
10. Code: no code copied from any reference or from the private Cold Synapse project; reuse only what the owner wrote and confirms free.
11. Age rating and content: war themes, civilian harm and dark humour need rating checks (PEGI, ESRB, USK) and store content rules.
12. Documents and provenance: keep dated design notes showing independent creation; they are the best evidence of original work.
13. Before publishing: strip Cold Synapse references and screenshots from files and git history, run a clearance search, and ideally
    have a lawyer review the title, the name and the licences.
